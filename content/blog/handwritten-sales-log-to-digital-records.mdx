Every trader has a version of this notebook: a log book page, or a stack of
invoices, filled in fast between customers. It works in the moment. The
problem shows up later, when that paper needs to become records: one sale at
a time, typed in, item by item, customer by customer.

Most people never do it. Not because they don't care about their records, but
because there is no time in a business day to re-type a page you already
wrote once by hand.

## Snap it instead of typing it

Open Zalyx Ledger, point your camera at the sales log book page or invoice,
and take a photo. Zalyx reads it and turns each line into its own sale (item,
price, quantity) the same way you'd have entered them by hand, without the
typing.

## You still check the work

The photo gets you a first pass, not a finished ledger. After a snap, every
sale it found is listed and editable:

- Assign a customer to a line, or leave it as a walk-in.
- Mark how much was actually paid: none, some, or all.
- Set a due date for anything still owed.

If a price is smudged or a name is unclear, you fix that one line. You are
correcting a draft, not typing the page from nothing.

## Why this matters more than it looks like

The hard part of bookkeeping was never writing the sale down once. It's doing
it a second time, in a format you can total up, search, and chase. Snapping
your log book or invoice removes that second pass. The page you already wrote
becomes the record, instead of becoming homework.
